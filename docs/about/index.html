Our story

It started with eleven people
and the Gospel of John.

Lamplight has been teaching the Bible verse by verse in the south metro since 2013. We're bigger now, but we're trying hard to stay the kind of church where people know your name.

  1. 2013

    A living room in Centennial

    Nate and Amy Whitaker open their home on Tuesday nights to read through the Gospel of John with a few neighbors. Eleven people come the first night. By spring, they're borrowing chairs.

  2. 2015

    The first Sunday

    On January 11, 2015, about 90 people gather in a rented middle-school cafeteria and open to Mark 1. We set up and tear down every week for four years.

  3. 2019

    A home on Orchard Road

    We buy a tired two-story office building just off I-25 and turn the ground floor into an auditorium and the second into kids rooms and classrooms. Volunteers do most of the demolition.

  4. 2020

    Streaming, then back together

    Like everyone, we learn to livestream in a week. We keep it after we reopen, and it's how many people first find us today.

  5. Today

    Still opening the Book

    Around 650 people on a Sunday, 1,037 messages taught verse by verse, and 18 home groups across the south metro. Same simple idea as that first Tuesday night.

What we're about

Four things you’ll notice
on your first Sunday.

  • Verse by verse

    We teach through whole books of the Bible, so we hear all of it, including the hard parts, and nobody's hobbyhorse sets the agenda.

  • Come as you are

    Jeans are fine. So are questions, doubts, and a messy week. Nobody will put you on the spot.

  • Simple, not shallow

    No fog machines or hype. Songs you can sing, teaching you can take home, and people who'll know your name.

  • Love first

    Right doctrine matters because it produces people who love God and their neighbors. That's the measure we hold ourselves to.

Elders

Led by a team,
not a personality.

Lamplight is led by a plurality of elders: two pastors and three men from the congregation who meet twice a month to pray, shepherd, and oversee the teaching and the budget. No one, including the lead pastor, has the final say alone.
